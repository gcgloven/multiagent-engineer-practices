# Engineering Design squad

Use this preset to develop a design and buildable roadmap. Apply the shared choice between roadmap planning and a quick MVP test. Lead coordinates Systems Engineer, Product Manager and Verifier. Software Engineer and Test & Code Review Engineer are available for scoped feasibility and testability work, or an explicitly authorized quick MVP. Planning-only work finishes with its artifacts and gated backlog. A selected quick MVP test instead uses the shared compact brief and bounded demonstration; a separate design squad or full roadmap is not a prerequisite for it.

## Starting brief and responsibilities

Read the need, current evidence, constraints and authority. Reuse accepted requirements and design. Record the selected mode, requested outputs and implementation boundary. The design loop below applies to roadmap planning and engineering design; the shared compact-brief route controls a quick MVP test.

- Systems Engineer owns technical design: behavior, responsibilities, interfaces, alternatives, failure/recovery paths, first implementation slice, and validation route. Use `design-solution` and relevant existing code/evidence.
- Product Manager can own/clarify the PRD and provides an independent explanatory/product perspective when useful. Use `frame-problem` to clarify the need, scope, behavior, and acceptance; use `explain-work` to explain the design, evidence, tradeoffs, and project fit. It can challenge unclear value or acceptance, but does not invent stakeholder feedback or technical approval. Its dispatch is optional; Lead must still assign a qualified PRD owner.
- Verifier independently assesses consequential design uncertainties or claims within a named review scope. It does not certify unbuilt implementation or production readiness. Do not add a review handoff solely to make every available member participate.
- Lead defaults to one Systems Engineer for a bounded scope/approach proposal, including a concise statement of need and acceptance. Add Product Manager only for a specific stakeholder/scope gap or a requested explanation, using the current proposal as input rather than commissioning another proposal. If stakeholder framing must come first, let Product Manager resolve that question before technical design. Parallel assignments require distinct outputs and clear ownership. Lead integrates the result once at the decision checkpoint instead of restating each return as another full proposal.

## Design and decision loop

1. Establish the design question and evidence boundary. For a new substantial system, first give the user a concrete scope/approach proposal under the shared human checkpoint: intended behavior, exclusions, credible alternatives, proposed engine/libraries or reuse, and consequential unresolved choices. Wait for that direction to be approved before elaborating its dependent architecture and roadmap. Do not build a detailed numerical or verification regime around a model the user has not selected. Distinguish observed behavior, adopted requirements, hypotheses, preferences, and unknowns. Carry research applicability limits into the proposed system behavior. Route a material missing foundation back to the relevant research/analysis owner or an explicitly human-authorized bounded experiment; do not silently promote a provisional result into a fact. An explicitly authorized empirical benchmark is a separate execution deliverable; a plan or metadata check does not satisfy it.
2. Produce or confirm the **PRD**: need and intended outcome; applicable canonical goals, stakeholder stories, functional requirements, domain rules, acceptance criteria and metrics; scope/non-goals; actual operating target; risks; and open questions classified as blocking or deferred. Record the Requirements gate and its evidence. Systems Engineer then develops the **architecture/design** tied to that PRD: representative successful and failure/recovery paths, responsibilities, interfaces/state, invariants, credible alternatives, ADRs for consequential choices, and validation. Depth must expose real decisions; a small bounded design can be concise without omitting required reasoning.
3. Add Product Manager only when its named audience, need, or tradeoff question remains unresolved. Ask it for that specific contribution against the existing proposal/version; do not ask both roles to independently draft the same scope and approach. A plain explanation can also be the entire requested outcome.
4. At the Architecture gate, distinguish blockers from accepted assumptions and deferred questions. Lead checks the proposal against the PRD. Request independent qualified review for consequential scientific foundations, substantial architectural risks, or a review explicitly required by the assignment. Review targets the proposed decision and available evidence, not test results that cannot exist before implementation. Then produce or confirm the **MVP roadmap and validation plan**: useful first end-to-end slice, next credible increments toward the intended production target, dependencies, acceptance/test/measurement evidence for relevant gates, resource boundaries, and revisit conditions. Record the technical Roadmap gate and present the numbered map at the separate human backlog checkpoint. Technical evidence decisions neither require every agent to participate nor substitute for human approval.
5. For a concrete blocking review finding, return the affected design to its author with evidence and a closure check, then reverify the changed decision. Apply the shared two unsuccessful repair-and-verification policy to that identified problem. Developing alternatives, refining a provisional frame, and learning from a valid negative probe are useful design work, not failed repairs.

## Ready for implementation

Advance the current slice only when:

- The exact PRD, architecture/design, and MVP-roadmap versions have passed the Requirements, Architecture, and Roadmap technical gates, and the applicable human scope/roadmap approvals and milestone execution authorization are recorded separately. They can be sections of one canonical document; each required output remains explicit.
- The intended behavior, scope, non-goals, and operating target are understood; the rationale connects to the actual need and available evidence.
- Critical responsibilities, interfaces, invariants, and relevant failure behavior are coherent enough to implement without guessing a consequential contract.
- The first useful end-to-end slice has observable acceptance and a feasible validation route, including comparison conditions when a measured improvement is claimed.
- Unknowns that block this slice are resolved or explicitly accepted by the authorized owner; deferred questions have a boundary or revisit condition. Unrelated open questions need not be closed.
- Required independent design/claim review is complete for the exact proposal, and the next implementation step fits the agreed authority and resources.

This gate means **ready to implement the named slice**. It does not mean code exists, tests passed, stakeholders approved an unstated choice, or a release is ready. If the request ends at design, return the design and this readiness assessment without starting implementation.

## Explain, hand off, or revisit

Return substantive, understandable design discussion covering the problem → proposed behavior → mechanism → alternatives/tradeoff → MVP slices → validation and intended operating target. Link the versioned PRD, architecture, roadmap, evidence, gate decisions, blocking/deferred unknowns, and next accountable action. Use the shared canonical prefixes for applicable records rather than alternative labels. Do not require a document per identifier.

Deliver the design explanation in chat, with the proposed behavior, decisive tradeoffs, relevant architecture excerpt or diagram, and remaining decisions. Keep canonical documents in the specified working directory and link the exact versions. Attach a focused diagram or evidence excerpt when it materially improves review; a bare link or whole-file upload is not a finished design discussion. If an explicitly human-authorized benchmark or another required deliverable remains unfinished, report the combined request as scoped partial work.

For a large roadmap, present the numbered child map for human approval before creating the required backlog children with dependency gates, owners, acceptance and budgets. Explicitly requested early draft children are proposed records only and do not bypass scope approval. When the named delivery milestone is authorized, activate its ready children through one supported route, carrying accepted design, evidence, human approval and unresolved history. A squad change alone does not require another issue or authorize execution. Keep detailed returns in their children and integrate milestones on the parent.

If later implementation or research disproves an assumption, reopen only the affected decision and its dependent evidence. Preserve sound work and prior finding history. Research, design, and delivery can be entered or revisited as needed; they are not an all-agent waterfall.

Implementation is assigned to Software Engineer and substantive code review to Test & Code Review Engineer. A design-only request does not activate those roles merely because they are available. Verifier reviews the named design/evidence question and cannot certify unbuilt code.
