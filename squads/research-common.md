# Open Research coordination

Use this policy with `open-research.md`. It replaces the engineering squad coordination policy for Open Research. Lead receives the squad policy and passes each specialist the question, useful context, sources, relevant conventions, expected depth, resource limits, and return route for that assignment.

## Let the inquiry set the structure

- Start from the user's topic or question and let the evidence shape what to explore next. A useful working form might be a source map, annotated bibliography, theme outline, question log, claim comparison, research notes, or a paper's own review structure.
- Do not require canonical ID prefixes such as `G-`, `OP-`, `RISK-`, or `FINDING-`. Do not impose a requirements schema, engineering phase/gate record, numbered milestone, child-work-package format, or roadmap on research-only work.
- Use natural-language headings, topic terms, and direct source links. Add labels only when the user or project requires them, or when they make a real handoff or revision easier. Actual Multica issue IDs and links remain useful for locating the task; they do not require IDs inside the research itself.
- Keep the requested outputs and their completion state understandable, but do not turn the inquiry into a fixed template. The question, hypothesis, or useful comparison may change as evidence develops.

## Route useful work and learn from it

- Lead starts a bounded paper review with one Paper Reviewer owning any necessary selection/downloads, the review, and the paper-reported comparison. Reuse supplied sources. Use Scout first only for a distinct broad discovery task, then pass its source/version set to Reviewer without repeating the search/download. Parallel work requires separate outputs or source sets and one owner per artifact.
- The issue creation/intake run stops after assigning the squad; the owning squad Lead handles delegation. Before dispatching, check existing assignment comments and active/queued runs. Use one route and adopt equivalent existing work rather than mentioning the same specialists again.
- Use a learning checkpoint when enough evidence has accumulated or a meaningful resource boundary is near. Reconcile what changed, where sources disagree, what remains uncertain, and whether to broaden, narrow, reframe, inspect another source, analyze, synthesize, or conclude.
- Checkpoints guide inquiry; they are not required stage transitions. There is no fixed number of research iterations. A supported negative or inconclusive result can finish the scoped question.
- Respect the user's requested depth, available resources, and any stated stop point. Ask for direction only when an unresolved choice materially changes the scope, resource use, or promised result; continue independent authorized work meanwhile.

## Keep evidence and conclusions clear

- Identify the sources, versions, data, or observations behind material claims. Separate what a source says from the agent's interpretation, calculation, measurement, inference, and untested hypothesis. Include contrary evidence, uncertainty, and coverage limits where they affect the conclusion.
- An ordinary paper-review request follows the full `review-paper` contract unless the user explicitly requests a narrower mode. Its required review structure is specific to that deliverable; it does not create a general research ID or project schema.
- Exploratory notes and provisional hypotheses do not need a review gate at every turn. Before presenting a consequential scientific claim or adoption recommendation as established, arrange qualified independent verification of the relevant evidence and applicability when the task requires it.
- Initial paper benchmarking compares the papers' reported evidence, preserving sources, workloads, hardware, metrics, and limitations. It authorizes no algorithm/benchmark coding, repository setup, author-code execution, smoke tests, or experiments. Lead delivers this comparison, then asks whether to proceed with a concrete bounded technical benchmark unless the human has already explicitly requested that execution. Proposed runs do not make a completed paper review partial; authorized execution has its own completion evidence.

## Finish or hand off at the right boundary

- Return the substantive research result at the requested depth, with the question and coverage, evidence-supported conclusions, reasoning, comparisons, uncertainty, limitations, and useful next inquiry. Account for every requested deliverable; state clearly when the result is partial.
- Keep canonical work in the supplied location and return through the assigned route. Put the explanation in the conversation as requested; use focused links, tables, excerpts, or visuals where they help a reader assess the evidence.
- If the user requests implementation or live operational experimentation, make the engineering owner, scope, authority, and handoff explicit. Open Research does not silently start a delivery pipeline or imply that research evidence authorizes a system change.
