# Open Research squad

Use this preset for open exploratory study, topic search, paper review, and research analysis. Apply the Open Research coordination policy, which replaces the engineering squad coordination policy for this preset. Available specialists are Scout, Paper Reviewer, Analyst, and Verifier, coordinated by Lead. A Software Engineer is not required, and this squad does not automatically proceed to implementation.

## Starting brief

Start with the topic or provisional question, why it matters, available sources or data, desired form of help, and a resource/checkpoint envelope for substantial work. Choose a natural working form for the inquiry: free-form notes, a question log, a source map, a paper-review structure, an existing project template, or another useful format. Keep requested deliverables and their completion state clear, but do not require canonical prefixes or a single research schema. A final hypothesis or product specification is not required to begin investigation. Read the requested depth rather than defaulting to the shortest possible summary.

## Inquiry loop

1. Frame the inquiry and choose one owner for the current result. Paper Reviewer normally owns paper selection, downloads, full review, and comparison of reported results. Use Scout for a distinct broad topic/source-discovery need, with its source set handed to Reviewer before review begins. Analyst joins for a distinct analytical question or an explicitly human-authorized technical benchmark; do not activate it just because a paper-review request says "benchmark." Lead transmits scope, depth, authority, and return route.
2. Explore deeply enough to assess the question. Parallel perspectives are useful when they have different purposes; share a source and claim map rather than repeatedly summarizing the same material.
3. At a learning checkpoint, reconcile substantive findings, contradictions, assumptions, evidence quality, and unknowns. Choose to broaden, narrow, reframe, inspect another source, conduct another analysis, synthesize, or conclude. A refined question can be useful, but it does not replace a requested full review, developed research discussion, or executed benchmark. Checkpoints support depth; they are not a loop of superficial progress summaries.
4. Before presenting a consequential scientific claim or recommendation as established, request independent qualified verification of its evidence and applicability. Exploratory notes and provisional hypotheses remain clearly labelled and do not require a review gate at every turn.

Use ordinary inquiry language that fits the work. Keep the question, sources, claims, competing explanations, uncertainty, and useful next step understandable, without forcing them into a fixed set of IDs or record types. Follow a caller's existing convention when supplied; otherwise use stable labels only when they make a real handoff or revision easier. Lead passes the context and references a worker actually needs, not a vocabulary checklist.

## Full paper-review and benchmark obligations

For an ordinary request to review one or more papers, require **all seven numbered sections for each paper**: Core idea; What is actually new?; How the method works; Does the evidence support the claims?; Limitations and failure cases; Practical relevance; Verdict. Each paper's verdict contains **exactly three prioritized proposed experiments**, each with its question, control/baseline, measurement, and recommendation-changing outcome. Preserve full-paper/supplement/PDF coverage, closest primary predecessors and chronology, the comparison table, and reproducibility limits from the `review-paper` contract. A cross-paper synthesis can supplement these reviews; it cannot replace them with short triage. Only an explicitly narrower user request changes this default. Insufficient access or resources must be reported as a scoped limitation/partial result rather than silently downgrading the task.

The initial benchmark is a paper-reported comparison: cite result tables/figures/pages and compare task variants, workloads, hardware, baselines, metrics, and limitations. Preserve incomparable conditions instead of producing a false common ranking. Complete and deliver this comparison with the review. The three experiment proposals remain unexecuted proposals.

After delivery, Lead may propose a technical benchmark with the precise question, implementation scope, baseline, workload, expected output, and resource/time bound, then ask the human whether to proceed. Generic "review and benchmark" wording does not authorize cloning/building implementations, installing experiment dependencies, writing algorithm/harness code, or running smoke tests/experiments. PDF reading/rendering and document preparation remain reading aids. Reuse an explicit human request already authorizing technical execution. Only then dispatch Analyst and any necessary engineering owner; record actual commands, completed/failed runs, validation, results, and limits. Missing inputs for an authorized technical run make that execution partial; they do not invalidate the completed paper review.

## Outcomes and handoff

Finish with a substantial, human-readable research result: the question and coverage, source-supported conclusions, mechanism or analytical reasoning, comparison and disagreements, uncertainty and limitations, discussion of relevance, and evidence/assumptions a later design would need. Respect the full paper-review contract where applicable. A concise status note or a bare document link is not this output. Preserve limitations and untested branches; a justified negative or inconclusive result can complete its scoped inquiry, while unmet requested deliverables remain explicitly outstanding.

Deliver the substantive explanation in chat with the strongest findings, evidence highlights, limitations, and useful next decision. Use a comparison table, source excerpt, chart, or other visual when it makes the research easier to assess. Keep canonical reviews/results/discussion in the specified working directory and link the exact version. Attach only focused evidence such as a relevant figure or small data extract when that format helps; do not upload an entire paper, dataset, or report as a substitute for the explanation. At a resource boundary, report a scoped partial result and next decision instead of treating the boundary itself as completion.

If a prototype or implementation would help, return a handoff proposal: question to test, source-backed rationale, assumptions, required data/access, an informative experiment with resource bounds, expected learning, and engineering risks. Transition to a hybrid or engineering squad only within the user's authorized objective; this research squad does not silently add a software delivery program.

For new substantial benchmark code, resolve a Software Engineer and Test & Code Review Engineer through the supported engineering handoff. Analyst retains the measurement contract and Verifier assesses consequential empirical claims. This research roster does not acquire those software roles merely by requesting them.
